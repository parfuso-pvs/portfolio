# PORT-402 - MEMX narrative chapters

## Objective

Turn the approved MEMX interview material into a readable case-study body without exposing private
systems or overstating individual ownership.

## Narrative sequence

1. The operating surface defines the users, controls, entitlement model, and eight-market scope.
2. Role evolution separates the frontend foundation, the market-technology scaling pivot, and later
   full-stack ownership.
3. The scaling decision explains how database configuration, market-specific server classes, and
   Phil's reusable frontend system worked together.
4. Production practice closes on investigation, review, manual testing, QA handoff, and team growth.

## Visual system

- A graphite market-scale panel establishes contrast without imitating an exchange interface.
- Ruled editorial rows make the three phases read as progression instead of generic cards.
- A blueprint surface contains the architecture decision while remaining an original abstraction.
- All high-variance desktop grids collapse into a linear mobile reading order.

## Accuracy and confidentiality

- Three MEMX-operated markets are identified as one equities and two options exchanges.
- Five market-technology customers are identified as one crypto and four equities exchanges.
- The initial database-driven architecture is attributed to Phil's manager.
- Phil's contribution is framed as the corresponding reusable frontend configuration system and his
  later full-stack feature ownership.
- No screenshots, reconstructed interfaces, order books, matching-engine displays, fake exchange
  data, or unsupported rollout percentages are used.

## Interaction states

The chapters are static server-rendered content. Loading, empty, error, success, hover, focus,
disabled, and active states do not apply within this ticket's new surface.

## Deferred

Original diagrams for feature-flag flow, entitlement layers, and WebSocket delivery remain the next
